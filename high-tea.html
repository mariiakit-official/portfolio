LEADERSHIP · I.C.STARS CYCLE 60

Leadership begins with how we show up for others.

High Tea brings leaders from across Chicago into conversation with our cohort. Hosting two of these conversations, and listening to many more, has been one of the most meaningful parts of growing my communication, confidence and leadership.

MY ROLE AS HOST

More than an introduction.

Hosting meant making each guest feel welcome, researching their background, preparing thoughtful questions, and creating a comfortable space for our cohort to learn and connect.

HOST · PATRICK ENGELKING

Curiosity as leadership

I researched Patrick’s experience in technology, innovation and business transformation, and practiced an introduction that showed both his accomplishments and his personality.

During the conversation I learned to listen carefully and let it develop naturally instead of simply reading from my prepared questions.

HOST · ARELY DORSEY

Authenticity and empathy

I prepared by learning about Arely’s connection to i.c.stars and her belief in helping people recognize their potential.

She spoke openly about family and vulnerable moments. As her host, I balanced a warm, personal introduction with respect for what she was comfortable sharing.

SEVEN LESSONS FROM OUR GUESTS

What stayed with me.

01 / GEORGE WANG

Great people make a difference

When you work with great people, you want to stay. A career is shaped by the people around you and the environment where you grow, not only by salary or titles.

02 / SONIA SINGH

Believe that you belong

I asked Sonia about imposter syndrome. Being the only woman in a room full of men, she reminded herself she was there for a reason: her knowledge, education and experience.

03 / VISHAL THAKKAR

Every team member matters

Vishal compared recognizing potential to American football: every player has a different strength and position, and every one matters, not just the quarterback.

04 / JILL WEININGER

The power of “yet”

“I don’t know how to do this” becomes “I don’t know how to do this yet.” My current abilities don’t define my future potential.

05 / ARELY DORSEY

Leadership includes vulnerability

Arely chose honesty over a perfect version of success. Leadership isn’t hiding difficult moments; it’s empathy, authenticity, and helping others see their potential.

06 / SCOTT BORUFF

Learn from mistakes

Scott shared the mistakes people make when they first work with clients. Each one is a chance to improve, ask better questions and build stronger relationships.

07 / PATRICK ENGELKING

Stay curious

Patrick’s love of reading reminded me that growth doesn’t end with a certification or a job offer. Learning has to stay part of life.

HOW HOSTING HELPED ME GROW

Not the loudest person in the room.

Before i.c.stars, I often felt uncomfortable speaking in front of unfamiliar people. Hosting pushed me to speak to a group, organize my thoughts, listen actively and respond in the moment. I learned that preparation makes it easier to be present rather than nervous.

Leadership is listening, asking meaningful questions, respecting different perspectives and creating opportunities for others. Hosting Arely especially reminded me that a thoughtful introduction begins with listening to someone’s story and treating their vulnerability with care.

Leadership begins with how we show up for others, even while we are still learning ourselves.
